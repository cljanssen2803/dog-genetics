# Stencil prompts — ready to paste

Eight images. Generate each one on its own. For the seven marking stencils,
attach the smooth body (`public/sprites/body-smooth.png`, or the original
pilot image) as a reference picture every time, so the shapes land on the
same dog in the same pose.

Every image: **1448 × 1086 pixels, transparent PNG.** (That is the size the
generator has been returning; keep it so the pieces line up.)

The dog's skeleton on that canvas, for reference:
- feet on the ground at about **y = 975**
- top of the back at about **y = 380**
- rump (where the tail joins) at about **x = 400, y = 515**
- top of the skull at about **x = 1080, y = 140**
- eye at about **x = 1165, y = 240**; nose tip at about **x = 1290, y = 270**

## Marking stencils — 7 images

Paste this preamble at the front of each of the seven prompts:

> Flat vector illustration. Use the attached picture of a white dog as the
> exact reference: same medium-build dog, same side profile facing right,
> same standing pose, same size and position on the canvas — but draw ONLY
> the white markings, as solid pure-white shapes with soft, slightly
> feathered, fur-like edges, on a fully transparent background. Draw nothing
> else: no body outline, no shading, no grey, no colour, no eyes, no nose, no
> ears, no tail, no ground, no text. The white shapes must sit exactly where
> they would fall on the reference dog. 1448 × 1086 canvas, transparent PNG
> with a real alpha channel.

Then add one of these:

**1. `mark-irish.png` — Irish white**
> The markings: a white blaze running down the front of the chest and up the
> throat, white on all four feet reaching just above the paws, and a small
> white tip at the end of where the tail would be. Nothing else. This is the
> classic Border Collie or Boston Terrier pattern, seen from the side.

**2. `mark-collar.png` — Irish white, extended**
> The markings: a full white collar wrapping right around the neck between
> the head and the shoulders, a white chest and throat, white on all four
> legs up to the elbow and the hock, a white belly, a narrow white blaze up
> the centre of the face from the nose to the forehead, and a white tail tip.
> Like a flashy Border Collie or Sheltie, seen from the side.

**3. `mark-piebald.png` — Piebald**
> The markings: white over roughly half the dog — the entire chest, throat,
> belly, all four legs, the neck and the muzzle — leaving large unmarked
> areas over the back, the base of the tail, the top of the head and around
> one eye. The edge between white and not-white should be irregular and
> patchy, like a Springer Spaniel or a Jack Russell. Seen from the side.

**4. `mark-extreme.png` — Extreme white**
> The markings: almost the entire dog white — body, legs, neck, head and
> tail — leaving only a single small unmarked patch over one ear and one
> small unmarked patch at the root of the tail. Like a mostly-white Bull
> Terrier or Dogo. Seen from the side.

**5. `mark-tan.png` — Tan points**
> Draw the TAN POINTS of a black-and-tan dog as the white shapes: a small
> spot above the eye (the eyebrow), the side of the muzzle and the cheek, a
> patch on each side of the chest, the lower half of all four legs from the
> elbow and hock down to the paws, and a patch under the base of the tail.
> Like a Rottweiler or Doberman, seen from the side.

**6. `mark-merle.png` — Merle**
> Draw the DARK PATCHES of a merle dog as the white shapes: many torn,
> ragged, irregular patches scattered over the entire body, neck, head and
> legs, in very different sizes — a few large ones the size of a hand, most
> small and jagged — covering roughly forty percent of the dog, with
> feathered, broken edges. Like an Australian Shepherd's merle patches. Seen
> from the side.

**7. `mark-brindle.png` — Brindle**
> Draw the STRIPES of a brindle dog as the white shapes: soft vertical
> stripes across the body, the neck, the upper legs and the side of the
> head, each stripe slightly wavy, tapering at the ends, of uneven width and
> spacing, with fur-like broken edges. Like a brindle Boxer or Greyhound.
> Seen from the side.

## Screw tail — 1 image

Same rules as the tail pieces already made (white fur with soft grey shading,
just the tail on an empty canvas). Paste this:

> Flat vector illustration of just a dog's tail, alone on a fully transparent
> background, 1448 × 1086 canvas. Pure white fur with soft light-grey cel
> shading only — no colour, no brown, no black, no outline. The tail is a
> short, thick, tightly kinked corkscrew stub, like an English Bulldog's or a
> French Bulldog's, about the size of a fist. Draw it as it would sit on the
> rump of a dog facing right: the root of the tail at about x = 400, y = 515
> on the canvas, the tail curling back and down over the rump behind it. No
> body, no background, no ground shadow, no text. Children's picture-book
> style, warm and appealing, not photorealistic.

Save as `tail-screw.png`.
